1What is communication?

Definition (slide)

Communication is a process that involves the transmission of meaningful information from one party to another through the use of shared symbols. It is successful when meaning is understood.

Where the word comes from

Latin “Communicare” = to impart, participate or transmit. Its root “Communis” = to make common or to share. So communication literally means making an idea common between two minds.

Communication is:

  1. The activity or process of sharing or exchanging ideas, feelings, information and experience between two or more persons;
  2. An act or instance of transmitting;
  3. The information actually communicated by some means.
Example

You text “ok” to a friend. If they read it as “fine, agreed”, communication worked. If they read it as “he’s angry”, information was sent but meaning was not shared, so it failed.

Remember it like this

Communis = common. Communication = making meaning common. Sending isn’t enough; the other person must understand.

2Managerial & business communication

Managerial communication

The process by which a manager shares ideas or information with other managers or team members. Done well or badly, it strongly affects staff morale, achievement of company goals and organizational culture. It is interaction between managers and subordinates (both ways) to ensure maximum productivity and peace at the workplace.

Stages of communication

  • Transmission of information, ideas and feelings.
  • Process: sender and receiver; how one receives, rightly or wrongly.
  • Correct interpretation and understanding is important for organizational effectiveness.
  • The greater the understanding, the easier it is to achieve goals.
William G. Scott’s definition

“Administrative communication is a process which involves the transmission and accurate replication of ideas ensured by feedback for the purpose of eliciting actions which will accomplish organizational goals.”

The 4 aspects in Scott’s definition

  1. Sender’s ability to transmit his own ideas accurately.
  2. Receiver’s mental ability to get the same idea: accurate replication.
  3. Feedback, the receiver’s response.
  4. Eliciting action that helps achieve the goals.
Remember it like this

Scott = “Send, Copy, Reply, Act.” The idea is sent, copied correctly into the receiver’s mind, confirmed by feedback, and turned into action.

3Four functions of communication

Communication = the transference and understanding of meaning. It does four jobs in a group or organization:

🎛️

1. Control

Controls member behaviour (rules, policies, reporting a complaint to your boss).

🔥

2. Motivation

Fosters motivation by clarifying what is to be done, how well, and how to improve.

💬

3. Emotional expression

A release for feelings: frustration, satisfaction, sharing with colleagues.

📊

4. Information

Provides the information needed to make decisions.

Remember it like this

C-M-E-I: “Can Mom Eat Ice-cream?” Control, Motivation, Emotional expression, Information.

4Process & elements of communication

6 steps of the process

  1. An idea arises in the sender’s mind.
  2. The sender encodes the idea into a message (words, pictures, gestures).
  3. The sender chooses a medium / channel (email, call, meeting).
  4. The receiver receives the message.
  5. The receiver decodes it: absorbs, understands, interprets.
  6. The receiver sends feedback (a response).

Elements

ElementMeaningExample
SenderPerson who starts the messageManager
EncodingTurning the idea into symbolsWriting “Meeting at 3 pm”
MessageThe actual content“Meeting at 3 pm”
ChannelThe medium usedSlack / email / notice board
DecodingReceiver interpreting it“3 pm today, I must attend”
ReceiverPerson the message is meant forTeam member
NoiseAnything that disturbs or distorts the messageBad network, jargon, stress, a noisy room
FeedbackReceiver’s response, closes the loop“Noted, will be there 👍”
Remember it like this

Sending a courier: you (sender) pack the gift (encode) into a box (message), give it to a courier company (channel), friend receives and opens it (receiver, decode), sends a “got it, thanks!” (feedback). Rain and potholes on the way = noise.

5One-way vs two-way communication

Two-way

Channels that allow feedback.

All discussions and dialogue.

One-way

Channels with no opportunity for feedback.

Announcements and memos.

Example

A class lecture with Q&A = two-way. A notice on the board saying “Exam postponed” = one-way.

6Need for communication

  • Managerial success depends on it.
  • There is a link between rank and communication skill needed: the higher the responsibility, the more time and ability needed for communication.
  • To achieve coordinated action.
  • To express feelings and emotions.
  • To share information about: (a) organizational goals, (b) task directions, (c) results of efforts, (d) decision making.
Remember it like this

The 4 things to share: “Goal, Task, Result, Decision” = what we want, what to do, how we did, what we choose next.

7Signs of unsuccessful vs successful communication

Unsuccessful

  • Not enough information shared
  • People confused and “in the dark”
  • Messages unclear and contradictory
  • No chance to ask for clarification
  • Inconsistent communication
  • Communication blocked; information lost in the channel

Successful

  • People feel they got enough information
  • Messages feel accurate and clear
  • People feel the manager will listen and understand their concerns
Remember it like this

Successful = “Enough, Clear, Heard.”

8How to achieve effective communication & its benefits

6 steps to effective communication

  1. Communicate for a purpose
  2. Compose the message with care
  3. Study the receiver
  4. Select the appropriate medium
  5. Provide feedback
  6. Act promptly on receiving feedback
Remember it like this

Like sending a birthday wish: know why you’re sending it, write it nicely, think about who it’s for, choose call or text, ask “did you like it?”, and respond to their reply.

1. Healthy relations

2. Better performance

3. High morale

4. Good image, unity

5. Reaching final goals

6. Smooth functioning

7. Greater efficiency

8. Higher productivity

9. Effective decision-making

10. Co-operation & co-ordination

PointMeaningSlide example
1. Sharing of informationMessages, letters, assignments, audio-visuals shared across departments and even with outsiders.“No Smoking” and “Switch off mobiles” signs in the central area.
2. PersuasionSkill to make the receiver understand and accept exactly what the information is about.A salesman persuading a customer to buy.

4. CoordinationActivities across departments are coordinated, so smooth communication is vital.Production and sales planning together.

Remember it like this

Think of a cricket captain: shares the plan (information), convinces a bowler to try one more over (persuasion), pats backs (motivation, morale), sets the field together (coordination), discusses with seniors (decision), gives clear orders, and listens to the keeper’s ideas (suggestion).

10Classification & purpose of communication

Intrapersonal

Talking to yourself, inner thinking.

“Should I study OB first or IR?”

Interpersonal

Between two people.

One-on-one with your manager.

Group

Among a small group.

Team meeting.

Mass

To a large public audience.

TV ad, newspaper.

Verbal

Using words, spoken or written.

Non-verbal

Without words: body, face, tone.

Meta communication

The meaning beyond the literal words. Slide note: sarcastic.

“Wow, great job!” said with an eye-roll actually means the opposite.

Remember it like this

By size: Intra (me) → Inter (two) → Group (few) → Mass (many). By form: Verbal, Non-verbal, Meta (hidden meaning).

Purpose of communication

To inform

Give facts. “The exam is at 10 am.”

To persuade

Change someone’s mind or action. “Please study with me tonight, it’ll help both of us.”

11Seven Cs of communication

CMeaningQuick example
1. CandidnessHonest, sincere, fairAdmit a delay instead of hiding it
2. ClarityEasy to understand, one clear idea“Submit by Friday 5 pm” not “submit soon-ish”
3. CompletenessAll needed information includedDate, time, venue and agenda all in the invite
4. ConcisenessShort, no unnecessary words3 lines instead of 3 paragraphs
5. ConcretenessSpecific facts and figures, not vague“Sales rose 12%” not “sales went up a lot”
6. CorrectnessAccurate facts, grammar, numbersRight name, right figures, no typos
7. CourtesyPolite and respectful“Could you please…” and “thank you”
Remember it like this

Your PDF’s order: Ca-Cl-Co-Co-Co-Co-Co. First two: Candid, Clear. Then five “Co” words: Complete, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Courteous.

Sentence: “Be honest and clear; give it all, keep it short, make it specific, get it right, say it nicely.”

13Skills for managing communication

Three skills: Presentation skills, Listening skills, Non-verbal communication skills (covered in topic 19).

Presentation skills: basic guidelines

  • Tailor the presentation to the audience’s needs.
  • Establish credibility if the audience doesn’t know your credentials.
  • Speak in a responsive, conversational style that engages listeners.
  • Presentation skills improve with practice.
  • Use visual aids to reinforce the spoken message.

Active listening: Do’s and Don’ts

Do

  • Create a supportive atmosphere
  • Listen for feelings as well as words
  • Note cues (body language, tone)
  • Occasionally test for understanding (“So you mean…?”)
  • Show acceptance and understanding
  • Ask exploratory, open-ended questions

Don’t

  • Try to change the other’s views
  • Solve the problem for the speaker
  • Give advice
  • Pass judgment
  • Explain or interpret their behaviour
  • Give false reassurances (“Don’t worry, it’ll all be fine”)
  • Attack if the speaker is hostile
  • Ask “why” about their feelings
Remember it like this

Active listening = being a good friend at 2 am: you don’t lecture, judge or fix; you listen, nod, and ask “what happened then?”

14Patterns of organizational communication

Downward ↓Upward ↑Horizontal / Lateral ↔
DirectionSuperior → subordinateSubordinate → superiorBetween peers / other departments
NatureDirectiveNon-directive; empowers employees to take part in decisionsOutside own chain of command; Henri Fayol’s “gangplank”
ExamplesOrders, instructions, speeches, manuals, handbooks, reports, newsletters, memosGrievance procedure, open-door policy, counseling, attitude questionnaires, exit interviews, participative techniques, ombudspersonSales head talking directly to production head

Purposes of downward

  1. To get the work done
  2. Prepare workforce for challenges
  3. Tackle misinformation and suspicion
  4. Build pride and confidence, boost morale
  5. Transmit work ethics and culture

Also: links people through information flow; gives job information, info for new employees, practices and procedures.

Purposes of upward

  1. Forward employee feedback
  2. Report on official matters
  3. Voice difficulties, grievances, complaints, demands
  4. Invite suggestions, creativity, participation
  5. Create a sense of belonging

Purposes of horizontal

  1. Exchange official information
  2. Build understanding and team spirit
  3. Solve difficulties
  4. Seek and extend co-operation
  5. Save time when quick decisions are needed
Vertical communication

Top management still takes the final decisions, but there is room for employee feedback: management interacts with employees, invites their participation, and creates ways to address their concerns, without giving up control. Downward + upward together.

Fayol’s gangplank (in simple words)

Normally, for Sales to talk to Production, the message would go up to the boss and then down again. Fayol said: put a plank (bridge) between them so they talk directly, saving time.

Remember it like this

Down = Do this. Up = Listen to us. Across = Let’s help each other.

15Informal communication: the Grapevine

Why the name?

A grapevine is the creeper on which grapes grow. With its tendrils, it can spread in any direction. Similarly, informal communication moves in any direction through informal groups, ignoring the official chart. (Gossip, rumours, “did you hear…?”)

Why the grapevine is important (why people use it)

  1. People need to share and agree on opinions and attitudes to feel they belong.
  2. People need to share their hopes and ambitions with superiors.
  3. People need to express emotions: joy, anger, hostility.
  4. The formal structure is rigid, so people bypass official channels to get and give information quickly.

Types of grapevine (slide list)

1. Chain

Message passes along a line of people.

2. Single strand chain

A tells B, B tells C, C tells D… one after another, in one line.

3. Gossip chain

One person tells everyone they can.

4. Probability chain

Information is passed randomly to whoever is around.

5. Cluster chain

One person tells a few selected people, each of whom tells a few more. The most common type.

6. Wheel

One central person communicates with everyone around them, like a hub.

The slide only lists the six names; the short descriptions are the standard meanings (Keith Davis) added to help you explain them.

Remember it like this

Single strand = a relay race. Gossip = one loudspeaker. Probability = random dice. Cluster = WhatsApp forwards to a few close groups.

16Managerial roles

🤝

Interpersonal roles

Act as leader and figurehead; interact with supervisors, subordinates, peers, suppliers, customers.

Time split (study): 45% with subordinates, 45% with people outside the unit, 10% with superiors.

📡

Informational roles

Seek information from subordinates, peers, suppliers, contacts; provide information to superiors, peers and outsiders.

⚖️

Decisional roles

Allocate resources, implement new projects, handle disturbances. Decisions depend on information from others, and must be communicated to others.

Remember it like this

“People, Info, Decide” = I-I-D: Interpersonal, Informational, Decisional. Time split 45-45-10: almost nobody’s time goes to the boss!

19Non-verbal communication

Non-verbal skills are essential for sending and decoding messages with emotional content.

Actions speak louder than words

Even when we don’t talk, we unconsciously let our mood, thoughts, attitude and emotions out.

Two types

Body language

Posture, gestures, face, eyes, touch, distance.

Paralanguage

How you say it, not what: tone, pitch, speed, volume, pauses, “hmm”, sighs.

“I’m fine” in a cheerful vs flat voice means two different things.

6 dimensions of non-verbal communication

🙌

Body movements & gestures

👀

Eye contact

🤝

Touch

🙂

Facial expressions

↔️

Physical distance

🔊

Tone of voice

Remember it like this

Go from top to bottom of your body: Eyes → Face → Voice → Body/gestures → Touch → Distance.

Eyes and face

  • Eyes are the most expressive part of the body: “the eyes are a window to the soul”.
  • The next source is the face: “a book where men may read strange matters”. It is highly flexible and reacts spontaneously. Through facial expressions we show respect for others, attitude, feelings and overall personality.

Five body-language types (postures from the slides)

🗿

The Rock of Gibraltar

Strong stable personality, confident, comfortable, good self-esteem, authoritative, leader type, awaiting a positive message.

Sits upright, still, open posture.

😎

Superiority

Overestimates himself, relaxed, casually waiting, won’t wait too long, showman, “let me see” attitude.

Leans back, one leg crossed, hand on chin.

😑

Boredom

Bored, tired, waiting, regretting coming, worried, anxious of outcome, “what is all this mess I see?”

Arms crossed, half-closed eyes.

😲

Astonishment

“Oh my god!”, “this can’t happen”, surprised, worried, “can this happen to me?”, “have I missed the boat?”, “I forgot something”.

Wide eyes, open mouth.

🙅

Defensive

“How is it my fault?”, “what can I do?”, helpless, all alone, “why blame me?”, “I’m hardly in the picture”.

Arms folded tight, body turned away.

Remember it like this

Five characters in an interview waiting room: Rock (calm leader), Show-off (superiority), Sleepy (boredom), Shocked (astonishment), Blamed (defensive). “R-S-S-S-B”.

20Minutes of meeting (practical)

What are minutes?

The official written record of a meeting: who attended, what was discussed, what was decided, and who will do what.

Format from the slide’s solved example

PartSlide example (Improving Communication Skills among Students)
Meeting (title)Improving Communication Skills among Students
Date, Time, Venue7 September 2026 · 11:00 a.m. · Seminar Hall
ChairpersonCommunication Skills Faculty
Members presentFaculty members and student representatives
Agenda1. Difficulty speaking English confidently 2. Low participation in class discussions 3. Fear of public speaking 4. Limited vocabulary 5. Improving presentation skills
Discussion & decisions / Action planWeekly speaking activities (faculty) · reps encourage classmates · vocabulary journals (students) · short presentations in continuous assessment · progress review after one month
Meeting concluded at12:00 noon

Remember the order

Title → When/Where → Who (chair, present) → Agenda → Decisions & Action plan (who does what) → End time → Signatures. Think: “What, When, Who, Why, Way forward, Wrap-up.”

Classroom activity situations (practice)

1. Conflict between team members (Riya & Mehul): sample minutes outline

Meeting: Resolving work-division conflict in Project X · Chair: Project Manager · Present: Riya, Mehul, Manager

Agenda: 1. Current division of work 2. Each member’s concerns 3. Impact on team performance 4. Fair re-allocation

Decisions / Action plan: Write a task list with a clear owner for every task · share progress in a weekly update visible to all · manager reviews workload balance every Friday · disagreements raised with the manager instead of arguing in front of the team · review after two weeks.

2. Declining employee motivation: sample minutes outline

Meeting: Addressing declining employee motivation · Chair: HR Manager · Present: HR team, department heads, employee representatives

Agenda: 1. Falling attendance 2. Missed deadlines 3. Employees feeling unappreciated 4. Possible solutions

Decisions / Action plan: Monthly recognition programme (esteem need, Herzberg motivator) · regular one-on-one feedback · anonymous survey to find what rewards employees value (valence) · link bonuses clearly to performance (instrumentality) · realistic deadlines set with the team · review attendance and deadline data after one month.
